## Working rules

Use synthetic inputs in tests, screenshots and releases. Real customer configs, reports, diagrams, credentials and unrestricted third-party stencil collections must stay outside this repository. The preview works locally. Keep import processing and layout independent of the update feed.

A site scope is based on configured devices and their linked APs, with explicit inclusion overrides. Hardware packs retain model assignments and optic choices. Keep hardware-stencil export tied to the active drawing. Device identities and SFP form factors are separate from physical proof. Preserve provenance and warnings when two records disagree. For artwork without a mapped jack, retain the schematic or boundary behavior and provide a manual mapping path.

Expand full-prefix ranges into individual ports before storing interface/VLAN state. AOS-Switch parsing follows the switchport builder: join tagged/untagged port lists that a backup wraps onto following lines (even inside a token), apply 'no untagged'/'no tagged', give trunk members the trunk's VLANs, drop the native VLAN from tagged membership, and mark a port's voice VLAN only when exactly one voice VLAN applies. Never turn unsupported ranges into fictional jacks. SonicWall EXP decoding is bounded and requires its settings header; only normalized topology fields are retained. Shared-media capabilities alone must not infer copper. For 90G/91G, explicit x1/x2 physical names select revision 2. Combo sockets retain one logical interface at the selected connector. Confirm 2920 rear cards and Cisco 3560-E X2/TwinGig adapters before assigning exact module jacks. Two-part Te uplinks must never alias copper access ports. Stack panel regions and escape lanes prevent leads from traversing another member; keep lane assignments separate within the available gap.

Preserve file and category selection when importing supplemental observations. Store normalized network records only, never raw command captures or configurations. Capture manual changes before recomposing evidence, and refresh the comparison snapshot after scope and hardware matching. Disabled sources must not leave stale observed fields, links or ghost peers. Preserve their known layout IDs across save/reopen. Configuration intent and current operational state remain distinct. Join a configured short hostname to an observed FQDN only when the selected evidence resolves a single compatible identity; retain domain collisions, explicit identities and original observations. Reopen imported projects through reopenEvidence. Accepted aliases migrate layout/order/report scopes and drawing keys, including named cable hashes, without deleting the old references or mutating source models. Existing canonical edits win on an initial merge; later source toggles carry the current active edits. Keep HA/management alternatives in review when a data-port observation distinguishes the same peer jack.

Artwork and port points must share the same master bounds and nested transform. Reject unsupported vector geometry rather than silently drawing partial hardware. Keep core devices in the saved order, allocate route clearance before packing, and preserve both endpoints through cross-page references. Cable geometry must stay orthogonal, avoid unrelated chassis/modules, keep painted parallel segments separate, and retain jumps at crossings. Changes to routing require geometric checks plus rendered inspection. After mandatory physical clearance, prefer the shortest reasonable clear route with small crossing/bend penalties; do not favor a page-wide detour just to remove a crossing or bend. Evaluate bounded pair-local candidates even when the initial route is clear. Use actual moved peer positions for departures, reserve stroke-width-aware lanes, and reuse the route bank rather than rebuilding every route for each side alternative. Preserve clear straight facing-port routes. Remove redundant collinear points without collapsing a reversal through a chassis. Fit automatic titles around cables; explicitly placed text remains an obstacle. Internal handoff captions must not create a padded collision at their own port departure. Keep core and wireless link legends accurate and independently editable.

Edit drawing uses direct dragging and Shift selection, with alignment, equal spacing and undo/redo. Devices move as groups; Alt selects native details. Preserve glued endpoints when moving a cable or its bends. Bend operations must remain orthogonal; removal can straighten adjacent elbows but cannot detach ports. Endpoint selectors validate physical aliases as well as interface names, reroute on save and retain styling. Generated handoffs become explicit only on a saved endpoint edit. A materialized handoff must suppress its former inferred connection. Native Visio connectors carry the planner's jumps as EllipticalArcTo rows through each apex (so they show on open) and keep the page arc-jump settings; Visio replaces that geometry, with its own jumps, only when it reroutes a connector. Legend samples stay straight. SVG hops include white clearance; a cable crossing a bundle of close parallel cables owns those jumps (otherwise the later cable jumps). layout.hopStyle chooses the look (Pages & layout, Line jumps): 'single' (default) draws one hop per crossed line, each sharing the room to its neighbors so a tight bundle shows small adjacent hops as Visio does; 'bundle' merges crossings closer than two jumps into one flatter bridge (half-ellipse, radii on the page axes). SVG, PDF, Visio and hit-testing use the same hops;  Include hop envelopes when placing core labels/titles. Legends derive only from styles used on their own sheet. Preserve port mapper coordinates through zoom and clamp selected groups together at the panel edge. Keep display filtering independent of the faceplate inventory. Imported native text retains weight/alignment in both exports. Include text moved outside a panel in label obstacles. Repeated hardware on different continued sheets needs distinct edit scopes, with compatibility fallback for older keys. A port mapping operation changes only the selected sockets and can be undone. History records project deltas so endpoint/inventory changes, settings and port edits can be restored together; keep unchanged stencil image strings out of repeated snapshots. Undo all retains redo order and loading starts a new session history. Native text-input undo must not trigger project undo. Commit inline text before file saving or another pointer action. Pointer capture retargets the native double-click to the canvas; retain the original pointer target to resolve the actual editable element. Double-clicking text bypasses its parent group and opens inline editing. Box selection uses page coordinates and selects top-level groups unless Alt requests details. Snapping starts disabled, is persisted per project and preserves attached ports. Arrow nudges use exact configured/modifier steps even when snapping is enabled. Master filtering must retain hidden selections.

Rejected inline edits must retain the connected editor, draft and visible error. Do not redraw the SVG on validation rollback when the existing scene already represents the valid state. Escape and detached-session cleanup must restore input. Bound label/title candidate generation to the visible page before allocating candidates, even when edited panel dimensions or anchors are extreme. Compact grouped port maps combine assignments across switches without dropping per-port peers, states or configured details; keep numeric range ordering and expanded/individual-row options. Port-number overlays share one measured box and font size across the panel, independent of jack form factor or digit count, and retain full interface names in metadata.

Automatic cables and legend bars share the scene standard width; Edit drawing must not override painted thickness with CSS. Visio LineWeight caches V in internal inches, even when U=PT. Write a point-valued F formula for explicit recalculation and check its agreement with V in the independent package validator. Preserve manual widths and native editability. The Microsoft MS-VSDX Document XML example documents the internal-inch cache: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/openspecs/sharepoint_protocols/ms-vsdx/ee70cff4-a87c-49d6-9957-39492dcc0c93 .

Cable type and speed remain link.media/link.speed metadata, independent of connector capacity. Preserve unknown imported values and explicit custom text; never persist selection sentinels. Changing cable class uses that class's style; same-endpoint metadata edits retain bend/name/width patches and are one project-history action. A newly materialized handoff needs scopeOverride=true so imported-site scope cannot hide it after save/reopen. Endpoint family identity and a hypervisor platform are separate from installed NIC/HBA port proof. Keep explicit inventory roles authoritative and generic symbols without fabricated socket anchors.

Diagram captions retain complete values. Shared textLayout measures SVG tspans and native Visio line breaks, preserving edit keys/full-value inline editing and rejecting impossible boxes below a readable minimum. Allocate module/table/header heights before packing, not after painting. Device-title placement uses measured wrapped bounds, never the unwrapped full-string width. Typed CORE_LAYOUT_CAPACITY errors let automatic core groups split while keeping handoffs with their owner; named page groups continue with their identity and connection preferences retained. Retain prior unsuffixed core edits as read-only aliases on continuation. Build a new scene before replacing tabs/SVG; failed same-project rebuilds retain the accepted scene and block stale exports. Device changes validate before history/model acceptance and restore their state on failure.

Retain bounded lazy modern archive loading and eligible picture-only legacy CFB masters; unsupported visible artwork requires installed Visio instead of partial rendering. The converter runs a copied file in a separate STA process before the UI mutex. Disable macros, events, refresh and workspace restoration. Keep client data and OEM collections outside the repository.

Do not change the release destination, automatic check cadence, or consent boundaries as an incidental refactor. Manual checks bypass automatic cooldown, show progress and a result, and can retry transient failures while respecting persisted GitHub rate-limit pauses. A lookup, a download, and an install are different operations. The desktop must see the initialized editor and a visible window before confirming an installed update. Native readiness failures need bounded host/navigation/resource/JavaScript diagnostics, never a weaker health predicate. The test-only update feed, diagnostic listeners and failure injector must remain absent from production builds.

For generated core layouts, preserve owner/handoff grouping and keep cable labels near their own route. Check rotated text against its rendered footprint. Visio cable legend samples are native 1D lines. Preserve shared line styles and same-page legend references when changing the exporter or the stroke validator.

Cable rhythm lives in ROUTE_STYLE (routing.mjs): standard device clearance on all four sides, a visible white gap between painted parallels, and shared rail and row pitches. After routing, neatChannels moves the movable runs (departure rows, rails and chassis exterior lanes) onto evenly pitched tracks per channel. It keeps the order of overlapping runs, keeps each block where the router put it inside the clear band (never centered in a wide gap), and leaves device title/detail bands clear when the gap allows. Untangle swaps track orders only when the spaced result has fewer crossings and no new obstruction or merged segment. Every change is validated, and a rejected change keeps the routed geometry. Chassis center tracks keep the visible gap and spread evenly; the next lead uses its column's exterior at the standard clearance and pitch, and only a chassis without exterior room falls back to tight center tracks. Module turns use the band with the most room per lead. Empty slots and the optional MM2 bay are `empty` regions: they open the way toward the peer only, never flip a lead away, and their painted captions stay obstacles. A socket whose only obstruction is an empty bay's caption leaves straight up: the caption slides along its bay to the nearest spot clear of every recorded rising cable (moveBayCaption, with its own native master key). Only when no spot is clear does the socket rise into the bay, turn under the caption and leave beside it (captionDeparture) instead of turning between module rows; lanes keep a full separation from every socket column and nest by distance from the caption edge, and an inner-row socket never rises through the row above. Cable type and speed text on lines is optional (layout.cableCaptions, off by default, in Pages & layout); the connection key names each color, port names stay, and a caption with an explicit drawing edit stays visible. When shown, a media caption that cannot sit beside a dense bundle becomes single-line inline connector text with a white background on its own cable, or, as a later candidate on a short run, the same lines stacked across the cable, instead of triggering caption-sized rail pitch. A final repair moves any row or rail left closer than the visible gap (or a rail closer than the standard clearance to equipment) to the nearest clear coordinate; it may trade one crossing for a merged segment. Collinear runs of different cables keep at least the standard clearance between their ends, and a repair never adds a run hugging equipment. A cable's rows and rail stay outside its own modular chassis, even through empty slots; equipment a cable does not connect to is solid, including the gaps between another stack's members. The repair also counts a cable's own socket leads, so leads from devices stacked above each other never overlap. Module turns keep a visible gap from socket edges (inset bands, a lone turn centered in its band) and fall back to the full band only when a busy module needs it; center/exterior bands or corridor channels that overlap keep their first placement instead of being re-spread separately. Unmatched ports on generic symbols take distinct perimeter slots. Even line spacing (layout.lineSpacing[page], 12-80, set through Organize connections) places every movable run of one channel key on a shared lattice at that pitch (centered in a closed gap, packed from the body on an open side); a group that does not fit keeps its routed placement, and every change is validated like the rest of neatChannels. Interior collinear spikes are collapsed; the first and last segments never are. Label tests may exempt only background-filled labels on their own path. Port labels never use leader lines unless the page has already re-planned for room (or is fixed paper); they may shrink to 0.76 of their size (not below 9) first. Port labels first look for a spot clearly nearest their own cable (every other cable at least 8 units farther) on the lead or first run after the cable leaves its equipment, or a leader callout near it, then any spot on the half of the visible cable nearer their own port (measured between where it leaves its equipment and enters the far one), including inline on their own cable with a white background, and only then any clear spot; inline candidates are bounded. Off-page connections end in one aligned column of white tags (far device and port in bold, then the page), never link numbers; tags share one width, stay left of every rail and off-page lane, are reserved in dynamic pages and on fixed paper wrap between words (three lines at most, else page only) and stack without overlap while touching their own cable.

Port numbers are plain bold text with no color block. readableOn picks black or white from the faceplate rect under the label; imported artwork keeps dark text. Role or VLAN color belongs on the socket. Booklet port maps follow Configuration Inspector's builder sheet (builderSheets): one whole device per sheet under a dark header block (NETWORK PORT MAP, the device name, the project name, models, details and interface counts). Each stack member is drawn as its own member view under a slim "Member N | model | socket counts" bar, at the common hardware width; members that do not fit continue on a "(continued)" sheet, and the shared planner's member ranges are not used for these sheets. Every socket is numbered; only configured ports carry palette fills, tagged dots (excluding native and voice), a voice diamond, LAG outlines, and black when stackLink or a stack/VSF role is recorded. Port maps follow the switchport builder's logic: every socket shows its VLAN data, a configured port without a native VLAN is white (No native VLAN), and a port or socket with none supplied is gray (Native not supplied), each keyed only when drawn. Every tagged VLAN gets a dot: rows fill the jack from the top, leaving room for the voice diamond, and dots never shrink below the builder's smallest marker (radius 1.5). When they still do not fit, the planner widens the common hardware for the whole family, growing a dynamic page. Fixed paper keeps readable dots, a '+' marker on the jack and a 'Tagged VLANs (+ on the jack)' list with the complete set. Chassis modules number their ports 1..n under a slot caption. The VLAN key runs down a right column (keyW). Trunks / LAGs and port descriptions go under it when they fit, otherwise under the hardware or on a continuation sheet; a sheet too narrow for readable numbers drops the column. A symbol key and safety note sit at the foot. bookletInventory writes one builder-style table per device: consecutive identical ports share a row with a count, an inferred assignment is shown muted when there is no description, and every interface address is listed under the mode. Expanded density keeps its detail table, and an explicitly chosen Combined presentation keeps the role-colored assignment matrix. The Automatic layout compares page counts against merged builder sheets (mergeSheets): the device's table starts under its own lists on the same sheet when a slim one-line bar, the heading and the first row fit, measured at the initial hardware width only, so text scale never changes hardware size. Keep portMap metadata (rowNames, portRecords, shown, total, overview, bookletRole) complete for the editor and filters. Wireless number leads measure the number text items (numberSide, numberBox, socketBounds); there are no separate number boxes.

## Build and verify

```sh
node scripts/check-repo.mjs
node --test
node scripts/build-web.mjs
```

```powershell
./scripts/build-windows.ps1
python scripts/package-release.py
```

The icon resource is a checked-in source asset needed by the Windows compiler, derived from the authored SVG. Do not replace it with a post-build EXE modification.

The root guard allows the four project guides, .gitignore, .github, desktop, docs, scripts, src and tests. Builds go under untracked dist, bin or obj directories. A new tracked root entry requires an explicit change to the guard and project guide. The dedicated root-guard test also exercises the filesystem fallback without a Git checkout.

Work on short-lived branches and use a pull request into main. The normal release is an intentional change to docs/releases/intent.json. Its version, tag and notes path must agree with the desktop project. Published assets are immutable. Future pushes, PRs, merges and releases need authorization for their own scope.

Shift cable/bend dragging retains a dominant axis until Shift is released; grid and guide snapping may only change the unlocked coordinate. Preserve Shift-click selection and Shift arrow nudges. Legend metadata edits prepare an immutable project, preserve mixed fields not explicitly changed, materialize inferred handoffs once and migrate cable/legend patches before a single validated history commit. Refresh directly overridden cable-detail values when metadata changes, retaining their position and text styling. Show used connection speeds in the edited legend, including mixed speeds. Never apply a selected speed to unrelated links merely because they share the destination style.

Trunk matrices measure full headings and row labels and plan column batches to minimize sheet count. Keep minimum column/row dimensions, fixed readable fonts, complete membership cells and footer clearance; do not return to a fixed six-column split. Unknown switch/firewall models use generic role symbols without socket anchors. The Netgate 2100 shared mvneta1 interface is not an individual physical LAN port. PA-415 management and ethernet1/1 are separate combo pairs.

Keep the desktop update strip hidden unless a newer release is offered. Its hidden state must reclaim the entire docked area. Preserve manual checking, update details and third-party notices through the desktop-only Help control in the existing header. Post status changes to the initialized editor without replacing its scene. Automatic no-update/error results must not create a permanent banner.

Size generated WAN cards from complete measured captions and the actual owner body width, including per-page scale. Reserve font-substitution clearance, keep cards inside the stack span and add rows for narrow bodies. Try compact placement first, then spread cards within that span only when cable/label clearance requires it. Consider short internal handoff corridors while retaining chassis and painted-segment clearance. Explicit card/device edits remain authoritative.

Built-in port modules use the Configuration Inspector reference style: gray optical faces, dark receptacles and dark DAC ends with a horizontal insert. The key adds a light frame as native editable geometry, with its own master distinct from the unframed socket overlay. Keep existing child indices via editSlot when inserting a frame, preserve legacy shared-SFP key edits, separate media variants in Visio masters, and retain selected local module artwork. Do not infer new hardware capabilities from this presentation. Reference: https://github.com/TechSavage1989/Configuration-Inspector/blob/main/webview/diagram.js .

Core pagination must try bounded padding and rail-clearance alternatives before splitting an automatic group. Preserve saved device order and readable label sizes. Preliminary capacity estimates must be optimistic lower bounds, not default-spacing totals. Transfer an overflowing owner and its handoffs to the next compatible automatic sheet so retries do not strand individual devices. Cache successful sheet renders only while their name, membership, fit and remote references are unchanged. Continuation badges sit outside passing cable rails; retain deliberate off-page edit behavior and bounded extreme-anchor searches. Regression coverage includes every ordering of a firewall/two-switch/two-WAN topology, denser chains, continuation groups and named-page persistence.

Adaptive core sidebars try 244, 208 and 176 units before automatic continuation. Scale spacing and module frames only within readable bounds; preserve full captions, native line weights, physical anchors and saved edits. Explicit sidebar geometry keeps the original sidebar width. VLAN/routing pages group records by device, keep whole sections together when possible, repeat headings on continuation and use bounded stable record keys independent of page/order. Retain legacy table caption edits.

Import connection coordinates in raw local shape space, then apply the same nested rotation/mirroring and final bounds as artwork. Preserve unnamed points with neutral labels, all distinct duplicate names, points in zero-height wrappers and points outside visible artwork by extending the shared frame. Never average sockets or silently drop an invalid cached point. Deleted legacy connection rows remain deleted. Picture-only binary readers must collect child connection rows; unsupported transformed binary artwork still requires Visio conversion.

User-facing requests for input, notices that work is waiting on the user, and published-release announcements must be bold and obvious. An emoji may be used sparingly for emphasis. Continue standing build/release authorization without asking again.

Device identity captions share manufacturer/model, platform and IP formatting; MAC addresses stay out of device captions (owner preference) and appear in the address panel and device editor. A wireless AP caption may name one interface MAC, never a count. Preserve imported address provenance and manual overrides; never infer a local MAC from learned ARP or forwarding-table entries. Prefer device identity, otherwise label the actual interface. Wireless fallback uses its connected interface. Measure full model/MAC captions before packing; keep bounded stable edit keys through reorder, pagination and save/reopen.

Automatic core layouts use a common faceplate width and preserve each profile/artwork aspect ratio, including tall chassis and multi-member stacks. Do not shrink individual devices to satisfy a fixed-height slot. A selected paper size may set a smaller common hardware width while retaining readable captions. Try bounded spacing/sidebar arrangements, then continue. Explicit saved sizing remains authoritative. Existing rendered pages, including named continuations, must be valid placement targets with assignments and off-page preferences preserved.

Stencil discovery searches selected local files only. Button labels and empty-result feedback must distinguish missing folders, vendor filename filtering and missing indexed masters. Disable browser context menus both in WebView2 settings and the browser app without changing keyboard editing behavior.


Page geometry is physical 96-dpi scene space. Keep a drawing default and per-family paper overrides through cleanLayout/history/save. Use measured page dimensions in every planner, SVG viewBox, footer, print CSS and native Visio sheet. Do not stretch the final SVG or silently shrink type to fit a new aspect ratio. Tables must rewrap before pagination. Hardware width must remain common across sidebar/spacing retries for the same paper. Stack spacing may tighten without changing member aspect ratios; oversized native stacks may continue by member while retaining inventory identity, local port membership and correct remote page references. Distinct member subsets/gap geometry need distinct native Visio masters. Never slice arbitrary imported artwork as if its members were known. Test all paper orientations, actual mixed-size PDF page boxes, save/reopen, undo/redo and large stack links.

The updater notice and review panel live in the editor; the native strip stays hidden and reclaims its space. Native bridge commands retain guards for check, download, busy state and installation consent. Render remote release notes as text, retain focus during progress updates, and keep a separate verifying state before install readiness. Check behavior and rollback policy must not change for presentation work.

Port-map and wireless planners must measure full hardware at a common width for their entire page family. Never fit separate chassis/stacks into fixed-height boxes. Try bounded shared scale alternatives and prefer larger hardware when page counts tie. Native stack member continuations retain original device identity, member-local ports and inherited LAG settings; use distinct member edit scopes and masters. Include actual panel anchors when assigning neighbor-only wireless links to members. Imported single-image hardware is indivisible. Explicit drawing edits remain authoritative. Core compact symbols must follow the common paper-dependent scale too.

The native lifecycle harness must isolate its deterministic HTTP fixture from live-feed cache state written by a restored production window. On the disposable Actions profile only, remove release-cache.json before fixture installation and deliberately seed a future pause to cover this boundary. Require a real fixture lookup and update offer before downloading. Never weaken production rate-limit pauses, readiness checks or rollback to make the harness pass.

Family-layout inference requires a complete port signature plus platform evidence. Keep family-only geometry distinct from exact SKU and optical speed claims. Declared identities supersede inferred families in either import order, while disabling evidence or manual edits retains normal provenance behavior. Reject NUL-bearing restore backups with readable-export guidance instead of extracting a misleading partial device. Manufacturer geometry corrections belong in researched-layouts.mjs with primary references; retain slot-qualified anchors and labeled unknown-member fallback artwork without fabricated sockets.

The update review uses the shared dialog with a scoped update-dialog class and a fixed action footer. Preserve the visible #close target for native input tests and reset styling when another dialog opens. Render release headings, bullets and inline emphasis with DOM text nodes only; never interpret remote HTML or activate release-note links. Keep notes scroll position and button focus through native progress refreshes. Do not promise restoration of unsaved projects; retain the native final save/restart confirmation and all existing download/install guards.

Dynamic / Fit all is the default only when a project has no explicit paper preset. Keep printPaper independent of the drawing default/overrides. Core dynamic groups must never use device-count or incident-link pagination; retain fixed-paper continuation as an explicit option. Fit bounds include nested artwork, cable geometry and negative manual coordinates without translating saved edits. Normalize only the exported Visio copy. Bound routing optimization and label candidate allocation for large pages. Device removal is one validated project-history action and must preserve evidence deletion patches; deleting an explicit WAN handoff must not regenerate its inferred duplicate. Use fictional data for capacity and UI checks.

Keep sidebar rows attached to the shared addOrderControls handler when changing inventory presentation. Dragging and Up/Down must remain available alongside Delete, filtering and Undo. Scope arrangement-dialog selectors to #dialog because the same ordering buttons also exist in the sidebar. Verify sidebar pointer dragging plus saved order, and retain the native sidebar regression checks.

Page display names must not replace stable drawing edit identities. Custom report scopes include their report IDs. Final drawing/panel transforms must carry inverse coordinates for move, align, resize and cable editing; keep normalized stencil coordinates and endpoint glue intact. Text-only scale must reserve table row spacing. Dynamic device titles may allocate space outside the existing bounds instead of requiring another core page. Common socket envelopes must not move model port centers or overlap adjacent sockets; preserve all catalog rows and imported artwork.

Dynamic cable-label retries must expand the actual rail gap as well as the canvas, using the measured caption height and reserving horizontal route space. Reserve initial body space from measured captions and the busiest local endpoint bank; retries may expand the canvas but must not move the body origin merely to make a rail longer. Include clearance in render-cache signatures; retain readable type, collision checks and endpoint glue. Wireless caption capacity checks apply only to fixed paper; a dynamic sheet grows around upper and lower banks. Reproduce full multi-file imports through all page families, since a core failure can mask a later report failure. Keep customer inputs and captures outside the repository and use fictional regression/export fixtures.

Keep logical VLAN/aggregate/subinterface observations in normalized evidence and review, but exclude them from physical core/wireless cable rendering and physical-peer occupancy conflicts. Unknown physical names still use boundary anchors. Long device MAC lists never change stored addresses; the explicit full-list panel and device editor retain every value. Shift modifiers must not trigger native double-click editing. Cable hit targets are transparent SVG-only interaction geometry, independent of painted/exported line width. Quick scale and placement must use inverse presentation coordinates and preserve port glue, undo and page identity.

WAN/ISP/egress cards sit above their owner by default and move with it on drag, nudge, align, Organize and Place beside (movementOffsets); a card with its own offset, or the placement reference, stays independent, and cards can be moved, placed and used as references like devices, including in Shift selections. A cable with saved bends whose two ends move by the same offset is translated whole. Place beside is a persistent edit-mode tool.
